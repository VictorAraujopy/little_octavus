# octavus

An octopus that learns to walk across the sea floor — and later to hunt a crab — through reinforcement learning. The brain and the PPO training loop are written from scratch in PyTorch; the body and the physics run on MuJoCo.

## How it works

- **The world** (`world_octavus/`): an octopus with 8 arms on a sandy sea floor, simulated in MuJoCo as sea water: Earth's gravity plus buoyancy (an octopus is ~5% denser than sea water, so only ~15 N of its weight rests on the floor), water drag, and slippery skin (friction 0.3) so the suckers do the gripping. Each arm is a soft tentacle: 16 segments tapering to the tip, each able to bend both ways, twist and stretch, held by springs so the arm sways in the water on its own. Like the octopus's muscle bands, a muscle doesn't move one joint but pulls a whole section: each arm has 4 sections with 4 muscles (bend up/down, bend sideways, twist, stretch/shorten) and the suckers of their segments, with a touch sensor on every segment. The arm stretches up to 70%, shortens 20% and pulls with 40 N, like a real octopus arm. The body is generated by `world_octavus/build_octopus.py`. A siphon adds 2 more commands, 162 in total: it aims the funnel and squirts the water in the mantle (up to 60 N). Like a real mantle it is a pump: a full squirt empties it in 1 s and it only refills while relaxed. And like a real octopus, whose main heart stops while it swims, it tires after about 3 s of jetting and needs 30 s of rest. The environment follows the Gymnasium API and places a target on the floor in a random direction every episode.
- **Energy** is the metabolic cost, what the food pays for: muscles pushing cost 4x their work (25% efficient), muscles braking about 1/1.2 of it, holding force costs about 10 W per kg of muscle at full force even when nothing moves, suckers hold for free (the tissue stores the tension), the jet costs its hydrodynamic power at the same efficiency, plus 2.7 W just to be alive.
- **The brain** (`brain_octavus/brain.py`): an actor-critic network with **921,273 parameters** (two hidden layers of 512). The actor is a single network that sees the whole octopus (304 numbers: where every muscle of every section is, how fast it moves, what it touches, plus the body) and drives all 162 commands at once, so the arms and the siphon can coordinate with each other. The critic looks at the same 304 numbers and estimates how much reward is still to come. (An earlier version shared one small network across the 8 arms, each arm deciding on its own; it learned fast but couldn't coordinate a proper gait.)
- **The reward** (`brain_octavus/reward.py`) is kept out of the environment on purpose: the trainer passes a `reward_fn(info)`, and the environment only reports the facts of each step. Octavus earns points for getting closer to the target and pays for the metabolic energy it spends, so it should find the cheapest way to get there, crawling or jetting. It also loses points for jerky moves, keeping its arm tips off the floor, spinning and not facing the target (it has a front, where its eyes point). Flipping over costs nothing extra and doesn't end the episode: it has to right itself, since ending it made dying early a way out whenever living scored negative. Each of those penalties was added after the agent found a way to exploit the previous reward. The penalties for bouncing, flying and lifting its body were dropped once the world became sea water with a real energy cost: the physics now decides whether swimming is worth it.
- **The training** (`brain_octavus/train.py`): PPO — play 2048 steps, score every decision against what the critic expected, nudge the arms towards the better-than-expected moves (never more than 20% per round), repeat.

## Running

Requires [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/) and Python 3.14.

```bash
uv sync

# train (saves octavus.pt every 10 rounds and continues from it if it exists; Ctrl+C to stop)
uv run brain_octavus/train.py

# watch the trained octopus (on macOS the viewer needs mjpython).
# It reloads octavus.pt every episode, so it can stay open while training runs.
uv run mjpython world_octavus/watch_octopus.py
uv run mjpython world_octavus/watch_octopus.py --explore   # with the random tries the trainer sees

# watch the untrained octopus flail around
uv run mjpython world_octavus/environment.py

# rebuild octopus.xml after changing the body in build_octopus.py
uv run python world_octavus/build_octopus.py

# open just the body in the MuJoCo viewer (the path must be absolute)
uv run python -m mujoco.viewer --mjcf="$PWD/world_octavus/octopus.xml"
```

`world_octavus/watch_pendulum.py` runs a simpler brain on Gymnasium's `InvertedPendulum-v5`, used as a quick sanity check: if training can't balance the pole, the bug is in the training code, not in the octopus.
